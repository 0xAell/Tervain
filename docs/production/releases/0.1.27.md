# Tervain 0.1.27 · The Bell Road

A broken hull, a missing blade and a silent mill now lead into the same story. Follow the notched stones from Grey Strand to Joss’s wagon. At Rillford, examine the dry channel and decide who should receive the spring water. The journal explains who needs your help and why. Its field guide and the map point to the same place. Exploring ahead changes the lead. Return to the wheel and the mill to see the consequences of your choice.

At the keeper’s hut, a split plank, a jammed beam and an iron repair each have their own appearance and sound. Turn the handwheel on the bank and the oak leaves rise and fall. A brass float behind the mill shows the new flow arriving over four game minutes. A shared rota puts the sun or moon on the keeper’s tablet. One bell calls the mill hands and two call the quarry.

The shrine, archive and village doorways use the wanderer as their scale reference. The six village homes have six different ground plans. Indigo cloth hangs above the weaver’s limewashed boards. Saw-cut oak, herb-drying bundles over flagstones, smoked boards and hides, a brick threshing floor and a slate brewhouse with iron drains distinguish the other five workshops. Roofs, timber, trees, ground cover and people share a pixel palette. Foot shadows stay on the ground when the player jumps or steps onto an object. A running jump carries momentum across the narrow quarry stream.

A Cut-road archer commits to a line of fire before releasing. A shield deserter turns to cover frontal blows. Sidestep past the shield, finish a sword chain with a thrust, parry, or shoot a fully drawn bow to open the guard. The warning ring and the HUD mark the same eight-frame parry window at 60 Hz. Drawing the bow preserves the traveller’s normal pixel scale. The ford crew has a separate job in the wagon tally. Clear three posts and return to Joss’s wagon for six coin and bread.

**I** opens the pack. Filter gear, supplies and quest items, inspect their effects, wear one faction charm, or bind an item to a belt slot. At the wagon, a hide can line the quiver for sixteen extra arrows. A piece of iron can hone the sword for four extra damage. **J** opens the journal and **M** opens the map.

Fourteen original arrangements carry a shared bell phrase from the shore to the village. After the hearing, Rillford gets a different piece for each verdict. The working mill earns a glass melody. When the quarry takes the water, a low viol leaves bars of silence at the empty mill. On the shared rota, flute and struck wood pass the tune back and forth. Seven stereo soundscapes follow the environment. Combat adds an immediate rhythmic accent, leaves space for impacts, and resolves when the last nearby opponent falls. Dialogue, music and effects have independent volume controls.

## Verification

The play gate exercised movement, the footbridge, the riverbank, two floors of a house and the bow at 1280 × 720 and 1440 × 900. Additional browser checks covered desktop, ultrawide, portrait and landscape touch layouts. A saved rota requires three witnesses. All three allocations produced their own aftermath text and the corresponding wearable reward at the mill. Mouse and keyboard checks operate the same wheel and gauge from dry ground. The gate passes through thirteen notches, and the downstream staff shows the rising water before the mill starts. Checking the staff again preserves the single charm earned. The acquisition chime and equipment banner identify a new charm. Automated rendering checks cover native combat poses and all eighteen village floors. Checks at 30, 60 and 120 Hz follow the struck body on each frame of the impact pause, including reduced motion. The bow sight closes to a continuous gold line at full draw.

The production build reports a 5,026.93 kB live JavaScript entry and 1,920.74 kB compressed. Its module graph includes the Rapier physics library. Three.js belongs to the separate 3D study. All twenty-one Bell Road tracks decoded in stereo and had distinct waveforms. The fourteen music loops have the same length so area changes can preserve musical time.
